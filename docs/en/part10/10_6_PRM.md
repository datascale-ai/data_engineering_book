# 项目六：CoT 推理数据集构建与 PRM 训练

## 本章概览

P06 聚焦把推理过程本身组织成可训练、可验证、可分析、可迭代的过程监督数据资产。章节重点不在单条思维链展示，而在 step 级监督、奖励分配和 PRM 训练接口之间的工程化衔接。

本章可以按四条主线理解：

* 种子任务与轨迹生成：从任务采样进入 CoT 轨迹构造。
* step 校验与奖励分配：围绕过程标签、reward bucket 和轨迹类型组织监督信号。
* PRM 封装与训练切分：把处理结果整理为可直接训练的过程监督数据。
* 评测与项目检查：通过指标、检查脚本和噪声分析验证数据工厂状态。

如果按工程顺序阅读，本章对应的是一条完整链路：

**任务采样 -> 轨迹生成 -> step 校验 -> reward 分配 -> PRM 封装 -> 训练切分 -> 数据评测 -> 项目检查**

这一结构对应的核心目标，是把 CoT 与 PRM 数据从结果监督扩展为围绕 step 级信号构建的过程监督流水线。

---

## 1. 项目背景：CoT 与 PRM 数据工厂的必要性

通用大模型在开放域生成任务上已经具备较强能力，但一旦进入数学求解、代码推理、复杂规划或多步判断场景，问题就会迅速暴露出来。

最常见的问题不是“模型不会说”，而是“模型虽然会说，却不会稳定地按正确过程推理”。

第一类问题是**结果正确但过程不可用**。模型可能给出正确答案，但中间推理只是模板化铺陈、后验解释甚至随机拼接。这样的轨迹如果直接拿来做监督，模型学到的不是推理能力，而是一种“正确答案的语言包装术”。

第二类问题是**过程看起来合理，但关键步骤已经错了**。这类样本对结果监督来说只会被归为“答错”，但对过程监督来说，真正重要的是定位：到底是条件识别错了、公式替换错了、代码执行错了，还是修复路径本身有问题。

第三类问题是**错误分布并不均匀**。正例轨迹往往更干净，而负例和修复轨迹更容易出现噪声、标注歧义和中间状态不一致。如果项目没有把这些轨迹拆开管理，就会导致 PRM 训练时信号污染。

因此，P06 的目标不是再做一个“带 CoT 的 SFT 小样本”，而是搭建一个**CoT 与 PRM 数据工厂**，把种子任务、轨迹生成、步骤标签、验证信号、过程奖励与训练接口组织成一条可复用、可检查、可扩展的生产线。

这条生产线服务的也不是一次性实验，而是一种更普适的方法论：

> 当团队未来需要把过程监督从数学和代码扩展到表格推理、工具调用、Agent 规划甚至复杂业务流程时，真正能被复用的不是某条思维链，而是这套“从任务到步骤监督”的工程方法。

## 2. 项目目标与边界

### 2.1 项目目标

本项目聚焦以下四个目标。

**目标一：建立从种子任务到 step 级监督的转化链路。**
也就是说，项目不是停留在题目和答案层，而是把多步推理轨迹显式生成出来，并进一步切解为可供 PRM 训练的 step 记录。

**目标二：建立可区分不同轨迹类型的过程数据体系。**
项目明确区分 positive、negative、repair 三类轨迹，使过程监督不再只有“对/错”两个粗粒度状态，而能表达更接近真实推理过程的数据分布。

**目标三：建立自动验证与 reward 分配闭环。**
过程监督的难点不在于生成，而在于筛选。项目通过规则检查、执行验证、结果比对与 reward bucket，将“轨迹质量”从主观判断尽可能转成可复核信号。

**目标四：输出训练侧可直接消费的 PRM 数据资产。**
最终交付不只是中间脚本，还包括 `prm_step_dataset.jsonl`、`train.jsonl`、`val.jsonl`、`smoke_test.jsonl`、`training_manifest.json` 等训练接口层产物。

### 2.2 项目边界

为了保持项目可复现性，本项目显式设置了若干边界。

#### 1）任务范围边界

当前项目只覆盖**数学**和**代码**两类推理任务。这两个领域适合先做过程监督示范，因为它们相对容易构造可验证信号，也更便于把错误步骤与最终结果对齐。

#### 2）轨迹类型边界

当前轨迹分为三类：

* positive
* negative
* repair

这样的设计已经足以展示过程监督的基本形态，但尚未覆盖更复杂的轨迹关系，例如多分支搜索、工具调用回退、规划-执行混合链路等。

#### 3）监督粒度边界

本项目强调 step 级 supervision，但主要仍聚焦于**规则校验 + 启发式评分 + 数据封装**，而不是完整展开一个大规模 PRM 模型训练平台。也就是说，它更像一个过程监督数据工厂雏形，而不是终局形态。

#### 4）规模边界

当前种子任务为 36 个，生成轨迹 108 条，总 step 数 534，规模可控，更适合作为方法展示和结构分析，而不是大规模工业训练库。现有数据已经足够说明流程、指标和噪声问题，但不能被夸大为“已经覆盖广泛推理场景”。

### 2.3 边界设定的作用

过程监督项目最容易出现的误判，就是看到有 CoT、有步骤标签、有 reward，就误以为已经做出了成熟的 PRM 数据体系。实际上，可信的数据工厂不是靠术语堆起来的，而是靠边界管理建立的。

对于 P06 来说，边界写清楚的价值在于：

* 让当前结论所依赖的任务域保持清楚；
* 避免把小规模验证结果误读为通用结论；
* 帮助后续扩展时明确优先级，而不是盲目扩量；
* 让项目更像一条可持续演进的工程能力，而不是一段概念展示。

## 3. 项目定位：P06 的能力链位置

如果把全书视作一条大模型数据工程能力链，那么 P06 位于“结果监督走向过程监督”这一段的核心位置。

前面的章节已经讨论过 SFT 数据、偏好对、评测与 QA 等通用问题。但一旦进入 reasoning 场景，团队会很快发现：

* 单纯结果标签无法表达中间错误；
* 单纯偏好比较不足以定位 step 级信号；
* 单纯“思维链展示”并不等于过程监督可训练；
* 训练前的数据验证，比训练本身更决定最终上限。

因此，本章的价值不在于重复介绍 PRM 概念，而在于把这些方法落回一个**可执行的工程项目**：CoT 推理数据集构建与 PRM 训练准备。

也就是说，本章不是在回答“PRM 是什么”，而是在回答更具体的问题：

* 过程监督数据到底应该如何被组织？
* 为什么 step 标签要早于 PRM 训练被设计？
* 为什么 negative 与 repair 轨迹不能混成一类坏样本？
* 为什么 reward bucket 不是锦上添花，而是后续训练接口的重要桥梁？
* 在有限资源下，怎样先做出一个可运行、可复核、可继续扩张的 PRM 数据闭环？

从这个意义上说，本章最重要的不是“多训练一个模型”，而是回答一个更大的问题：

> 当团队想让模型学会更好的过程，而不是只学会更好的结果时，数据工程应该怎样重新设计？

## 4. 整体架构：从种子任务到 PRM 训练资产的过程监督流水线

从工程视角看，本项目可以拆成三层。

### 4.1 第一层：任务与轨迹生成层

这一层解决的是“如何稳定获得可分析的推理轨迹”。主要包括：

* 任务采样
* 规格定义
* 多轨迹生成
* 轨迹类型控制
* 正负修复样本配比

这一层的目标不是马上输出训练集，而是把原始任务扩展成一组可比较、可分析、可验证的 reasoning traces。

### 4.2 第二层：步骤验证与奖励分配层

这一层解决的是“这些过程值不值得学”。主要包括：

* step segmentation
* Rule checking
* Result execution and comparison
* step label generation
* reward bucket allocation
* process-only signal extraction

This is the most critical part of the entire project, because it determines whether the system learns whether it can "write a thinking chain" or "accumulate reliable process signals."

### 4.3 The third layer: training encapsulation and delivery layer

This layer addresses "whether these process data can be directly consumed by the training and evaluation system." Mainly include:

* PRM step data encapsulation
* train/val segmentation
* smoke test construction
* manifest generation
*Dataset evaluation
* Project check script

At this point, the project went from "generating some reasoning traces" to "building a process supervision data pipeline."

## 5. Seed task: the task layer serves as the starting point for supervision

When many people talk about PRM, they will focus all their attention on the reward or scoring model, but ignore the upstream task layer design.

But in real engineering, the upper limit of PRM training depends largely on: **What task, difficulty, and type of process you let the system see first. **

### 5.1 Why seed tasks are necessary

Without a clear task pool, the project can easily degenerate into "randomly generating some reasoning traces", which may seem like a lot, but are actually chaotically distributed and unevenly difficult, and cannot explain why these steps are worthy of supervision.

After making the task layer explicit, the project can:

* Constrain the source and specifications of the questions;
* Maintain a basic balance among different task domains;
* Track which seed a certain track comes from;
* After training, check whether the problem lies in the task, trajectory or verification process.

### 5.2 Why choose mathematics and code first for the current project?

Mathematics and coding are two great starting points in process supervision.

On the one hand, they all have relatively clear correctness standards, which facilitates result comparison and automatic verification; on the other hand, they have strong enough multi-step reasoning properties to truly expose step-level errors, rather than simply classifying right and wrong.

### 5.3 Why the seed task is not equal to the final training sample

The seed task is only a layer of "supervision source", not the final supervision unit. The real value of P06 is that it expands the seed task into multiple trajectories, then cuts the trajectory into steps, and finally exports PRM data. This link means:

> The project focuses not on the topic itself, but on the process signals generated around the topic.

## 6. Task sampling and specification design: pre-scheduling layer

`sampler.py` is responsible for constructing two types of seeds from GSM8K and MBPP at this layer: the math task extracts the final answer and splits it into reference steps, the code task retains `reference_code`, `test_setup_code` and `test_list`, and finally falls to `seed_pool.jsonl` and `task_spec.json`. This way the required fields for subsequent trace generation and verification can be prepared at the upstream stage.

The corresponding implementation is as follows:```python
for index, record in enumerate(gsm8k):
    final_answer = extract_final_answer(record["answer"])
    steps = split_reasoning_steps(record["answer"])
    if not final_answer or len(steps) < 2:
        continue
    seeds.append(
        {
            "seed_id": f"math_{index}",
            "domain": "math",
            "topic": infer_math_topic(record["question"]),
            "question": record["question"],
            "reference_steps": steps,
            "final_answer": final_answer,
            "source_dataset": "gsm8k_train",
        }
    )
````task_spec` writes project constraints as structured configuration:```python
task_spec = {
    "seed_count": len(seeds),
    "domain_distribution": dict(Counter(seed["domain"] for seed in seeds)),
    "trace_targets": {
        "positive": "correct reasoning trace",
        "negative": "corrupted or wrong reasoning trace",
        "repair": "wrong step followed by correction",
    },
    "validation_targets": [
        "final_answer_match",
        "step_level_quality_labels",
        "code_execution_and_unit_tests",
    ],
}
```The first step in the project process is `src/sampler.py`: first sample tasks and generate specifications, and then enter inference trajectory generation. P06 Treat “data distribution” from the beginning as an engineering object that needs to be managed explicitly, rather than leaving it to model randomness.

### 6.1 What problems does specification design solve?

The so-called task spec is essentially answering several questions:

* Is the current task mathematics or coding?
* What is the approximate difficulty range of the questions?
* Is a specific structure required for trajectory generation?
* Does subsequent verification rely first on rules, execution or result comparison?

By forwarding these issues to the specification layer, subsequent project generation, verification, and analysis will have unified boundaries.

### 6.2 Why sampling can’t just be “diversified”

Many projects say they have done sampling, but in fact they just randomly select questions. But for PRM, random does not equal reasonable. Because what the process supervision is more concerned about is not the surface variety of the questions, but:

* Whether the error types are rich enough;
* Whether the step granularity is convenient for segmentation;
* Verify whether the signal is available;
* Is there a basis for comparison between positive and negative repair trajectories?

### 6.3 Engineering value of task spec

The current project clearly outputs `seed_pool.jsonl` and `task_spec.json`, which shows that it does not hide the task definition in the code, but explicitly puts the upstream scheduling information on the disk. The benefits of doing this are:

* The impact of different specifications on trajectory quality can be compared later;
* You can track whether certain types of tasks are more likely to generate noise during review;
* The task layer and trajectory layer can be iterated separately instead of rewriting the entire chain each time;
* Interfaces can be reserved for future expansion into new task domains.

## 7. Trajectory generation: parallel construction of positive, negative, repair

The three types of trajectories of P06 are explicitly constructed in `generate_traces.py`: positive examples directly use the reference step; negative examples create errors by tampering with key figures or mutating reference codes; repair trajectories append a correction step after the error trajectory. This implementation implements "process supervision" into actionable data synthesis logic.

The corresponding implementation is as follows:```python
wrong_steps = [dict(step) for step in correct_steps]
wrong_steps[-1]["text"] = corrupt_numeric_text(wrong_steps[-1]["text"])
wrong_steps[-1]["label"] = 0

negative_final = {
    "step_idx": len(wrong_steps) + 1,
    "text": f"Final answer: {corrupt_numeric_text(seed['final_answer'])}",
    "label": 0,
    "kind": "final",
}

repair_steps = wrong_steps + [negative_final]
repair_steps.append(
    {
        "step_idx": len(repair_steps) + 1,
        "text": f"Correction: the previous arithmetic was wrong. The correct final answer is {seed['final_answer']}.",
        "label": 1,
        "kind": "repair",
    }
)
```This implementation shows that the repair trace is not a new question constructed separately, but a repair step is explicitly added after the negative trace. The implementation logic of code tasks is similar, except that errors come from `mutate_python_code`, and verification relies on subsequent unit test execution.

One of the core processes of P06 is `src/generate_traces.py`, which is to generate multiple reasoning trajectories based on seed tasks instead of only retaining one standard answer trajectory.

### 7.1 Why can’t we only generate positive trajectories?

If the process supervision only retains positive trajectories, the model can indeed learn "what the correct-looking process looks like", but it still cannot effectively distinguish:

* Which partial steps are unreliable;
* Which repair methods are effective;
* Which errors will be amplified in subsequent steps.

This can lead to a PRM that can easily degenerate into a scorer that favors lengthy and neatly formatted trajectories rather than a model that can identify process quality.

### 7.2 What problem does the negative trajectory solve?

The value of a negative trajectory is that it provides an explicit frame of reference for the "error process". Through it, the system can learn:

* How intermediate steps deviate from the correct path;
* What are the precursors before final failure;
* Which errors are just local deviations, and which errors will cause the entire link to collapse.

### 7.3 Why repair trajectory is more important and more dangerous

The repair trajectory is closer to the inference revision process in the real world than ordinary negative examples. It helps the system learn:

* How to return to the correct path after an error occurs;
* What types of remedies are effective;
* What context should be preserved during the repair process.

But the repair trajectory is also the most likely to introduce noise. Because if the repair logic itself is unstable, what the model learns may not be "how to correct errors", but "how to repackage errors into seemingly reasonable continued reasoning."

### 7.4 What does the trajectory structure of the current project indicate?

Existing indicators show that the project generated 108 trajectories, of which three types of trajectories are completely symmetrical: `positive=36`, `negative=36`, `repair=36`. This shows that the trajectory structure of the project is not to temporarily generate several samples, but to clearly design the three types of processes as parallel supervision objects.

## 8. Step segmentation and schema: the smallest unit of process supervision

After completing the trajectory generation, the project will not directly use the entire reasoning trace for training, but first perform step-level segmentation. This step is one of the most essential differences between P06 and ordinary CoT sample libraries.

### 8.1 Why step is a necessary unit

The entire trajectory can only express "whether it is good or not in general", but cannot express "specifically which step is good and which step is bad".

What PRM wants to learn is precisely this more fine-grained judgment ability:

* Whether a certain step is logically coherent;
* Whether a certain step is consistent with the previous step;
* Whether a certain step introduced wrong facts or wrong codes;
* Whether a certain step has raised a red flag even if it did not directly lead to the final failure.

### 8.2 What problem does schema solve here?

The so-called step schema is not to make the fields more complete, but to allow verification, statistics and training to all work around the same unit. A typical step record should at least contain:

* `trace_id`
* `task_id`
* `domain`
* `trace_type`
* `step_index`
* `step_text`
* `step_label`
* `reward_bucket`
* `validation_flags`
* `final_outcome`
* `metadata`

With this layer of schema, the project can be followed up with:

* Analyze the quality differences of steps in different domains;
* Compare the noise structure of different trace types;
* Track what step distribution a certain reward bucket comes from;
* Directly encapsulate step data into PRM training interface.

### 8.3 Why step schema must be kept separately

Because many process supervision projects fail, it is not because the model is too poor, but because the minimum supervision unit is not clearly designed from the beginning, resulting in:

* During training, only the entire answer can be scored;
* Unable to review specific steps when an error occurs;
* Data segmentation can only be roughly processed at the sample level;
* There is no gripper for noise analysis.

From this perspective, step schema is not ancillary design, but the foundation of the PRM data factory.

## 9. Automatic verification: verification of results of process supervision

`validate_and_score.py` is responsible for stringing together domain routing, result verification, `trace_score` calculation and reward bucket mapping at this layer: final answer matching for math tasks, code execution and unit testing for coding tasks. In this way, automatic verification can be implemented as a complete closed-loop project.

The corresponding implementation is as follows:```python
if trace["domain"] == "math":
    passed, validation = validate_math_trace(trace)
else:
    passed, validation = validate_code_trace(trace)

label_sum = sum(step["label"] for step in trace["steps"])
score = label_sum / max(1, len(trace["steps"]))
bucket = reward_bucket(score)

enriched["validation_passed"] = passed
enriched["trace_score"] = round(score, 4)
enriched["reward_bucket"] = bucket
```Among them, the reward bucket is not a black box score, but a segmented function that can be interpreted by rules:```python
def reward_bucket(score: float) -> str:
    if score >= 0.95:
        return "high"
    if score >= 0.6:
        return "medium"
    if score > 0:
        return "low"
    return "zero"
```The advantage of writing this way is that the signals that the verification relies on, the source of the bucket, and the relationship between them become clear.

The intermediate core step of P06 is `src/validate_and_score.py`, which is to verify, score and assign labels to the generated trajectories. This step is very critical because it determines whether the project is accumulating supervision signals or amplifying generated noise.

### 9.1 Why verification must be preceded by the data production process

Many teams understand verification as post-training evaluation, but process supervision is not like this. Because once dirty trajectories enter the PRM data, the model trained later is likely to be better at identifying "pseudo-inference patterns" rather than being better at identifying the true process quality.

Therefore, verification here is not the end of the project, but part of the production line.

### 9.2 What signals can automatic verification rely on?

Judging from the existing process description, the project will combine **rule checking, execution and result comparison** to assign labels and rewards to trajectories and steps. This shows that the current verification does not stay on the surface of the text, but tries to combine formal correctness, executability and result consistency.

In mathematical tasks, this verification is more likely to rely on result comparison; in code tasks, it is more suitable to combine execution results, test samples, or syntax/behavior checks. This design makes "step quality" no longer just a subjective description.

### 9.3 Why is verification not as strict as possible?

Intuitively, many people feel that as long as the rules are tightened, cleaner data can be obtained. However, in process supervision projects, too strict verification can also cause problems:

* Valuable intermediate errors were excessively removed;
* repair track loses its meaning;
* The data distribution is dominated by positive examples, and it is difficult for PRM to learn the error correction boundary;
* Project becomes outcome-only variant.

Therefore, a more reasonable strategy is not to "cleanse across the board" but to preserve structured differences: which steps passed, which steps failed, and which steps failed but are still worth retaining as process signals.

### 9.4 What does the verification result of the current project indicate?

Existing indicators show that the overall trajectory verification pass rate is `67.59%`, but the positive trajectory pass rate reaches `100.00%`, indicating that the current problem is mainly focused on the control of negative and repair trajectories. This result is very valuable because it clearly points out that subsequent optimization of the project should not blindly expand the volume, but should prioritize improving the quality of cleaning and verification.

## 10. Step label design: operationalization of process fields

When many people talk about process supervision, they use some abstract concepts, such as "high-quality thinking chain", "credible steps" and "process consistency". But what can really be put into practice in the project is not these abstract words, but the data labels that can be entered into fields, statistics and training.

### 10.1 The role of step label

The value of step label is that it breaks down "process quality" into machine-consumable supervision signals. Through tags, projects can identify:

* Which steps should be strengthened;
* Which steps should be punished;
* Which steps are process-specific signals rather than appendages to the outcome;
* Which steps are in the negative trajectory, but are still locally valuable.

### 10.2 Why tags cannot only be used for binary classification

Simple positive and negative classification is certainly attractive because it is simple to implement and intuitive to train. But this is often too crude for PRM. The reason is:

* A repair track may have an error in the first half and a correction in the second half;
* A negative trajectory may have only one critical error;
* Some steps, while not optimal, should not be equated to pure noise.

Therefore, P06 introduces the joint structure of step label and reward bucket, which essentially avoids rudely compressing all complexity into one label bit.

### 10.3 Why is process-only supervision signal important?

Existing indicators show that there are `144` process-only supervision signal steps in the project. The engineering implications of this number are clear: process monitoring does provide additional signals that outcome-only alternatives cannot replace. If you only look at the end result, the value of these 144 steps is simply ignored.

This just shows that the PRM data factory is not "just taking apart the answers", but building a new supervisory layer beyond outcome.

## 11. Reward bucket: hierarchical scoring mechanism

When many teams do process scoring, they tend to simplify the entire problem into "giving a score to the trajectory." Of course, this approach can run through quickly, but it will also quickly encounter problems: the scores are too sparse, the boundaries are too subjective, and the training interface is unstable.

### 11.1 Engineering significance of reward bucket

The value of reward bucket is not that it is more "advanced" than continuous fractions, but that it is more suitable for expressing interpretable intervals in the early engineering stages. With buckets, teams can more clearly differentiate between:

* A high-quality process that clearly deserves reinforcement;
* An intermediate process that has certain value but is not stable enough;
* Low-quality processes that should be downgraded or even eliminated.

### 11.2 Why bucket is more stable than continuous fractions

In small-scale process supervision projects, continuous scores often cause two problems:

* The scoring caliber is difficult to stabilize;
* It is subsequently difficult to explain where the difference between 0.73 and 0.78 comes from.

In contrast, buckets make it easier for a project to:

* The rules are clearly defined;
* Distribution statistics are straightforward;
* Training mapping is simple;
* Easier to locate abnormal samples during review.

### 11.3 What does the bucket structure of the current project indicate?

The existing reward bucket distribution is: `high=36`, `medium=45`, `low=27`. This distribution means at least two things:

First, rather than making all trajectories “averagely good”, the project creates distinguishable hierarchies of quality.
Second, the current data are more biased toward medium- and high-quality processes, but still retain enough low-quality samples to provide a basis for comparative learning for PRM.

## 12. PRM data encapsulation: training interface layer

This section emphasizes the actual structure of the step-level dataset. P06 does not copy the entire trace directly to the training directory, but reorganizes each step into a PRM training record, and then generates `train.jsonl`, `val.jsonl`, `smoke_test.jsonl` and `training_manifest.json`. This shows that the smallest unit consumed by the training system is not questions, but step records with labels and rewards.

The corresponding packaging structure is as follows:```python
record = {
    "record_id": f"{trace_id}_step_{step_idx}",
    "domain": domain,
    "trace_type": trace_type,
    "prompt": question,
    "step_text": step_text,
    "label": step_label,
    "reward_bucket": reward_bucket,
}
```With this paragraph, the main text will be more like a project implementation rather than a summary of results.

After completing trajectory generation, step segmentation, verification and reward distribution, the project still needs to complete an often overlooked step: repackaging these intermediate signals into a data format that can be directly consumed by the training system.

In the process of P06, this step is responsible for `src/prepare_prm_data.py`. Its significance is that what the project ultimately builds is not a bunch of scattered intermediate files, but a set of truly trainable data assets.

### 12.1 Why the encapsulation layer is important

If there is no encapsulation layer, the project will have a common fault:

* The research side says "We already have step labels";
* The training side says "I cannot use these files directly";
* The reviewer said "I don't know how train/val is cut".

The role of the encapsulation layer is to reorganize the previous complex process supervision signals into standard assets that can enter the training and evaluation system.

### 12.2 What key training products are output by the current project?

Existing reports show that the project has clearly output:

* `data/training/prm_step_dataset.jsonl`
* `data/training/train.jsonl`
* `data/training/val.jsonl`
* `data/training/smoke_test.jsonl`
* `data/training/training_manifest.json`

This shows that the project has not stayed at the "analysis trajectory" stage, but has completed the implementation of the training interface layer.

### 12.3 Why smoke test and manifest must also exist

Many data projects will focus on train/val but ignore smoke test and manifest. In fact, these two items are very important.

* `smoke_test.jsonl` indicates that the project considers rapid verification before training;
* `training_manifest.json` indicates that the project considers data version, scale, segmentation and meta-information management.

Such products will not directly improve the model score, but will significantly improve the maintainability and reproducibility of the project.

## 15. Evaluation and project inspection: self-inspection layer

`run_p6_checks.py` Write a set of specific project access control as automatic checks: first run `py_compile` and `evaluate_prm.py`, and then check whether the required files exist, whether both math/code domains appear, whether the three types of positive/negative/repair trajectories are covered, whether the step label covers both positive and negative categories, whether the reward bucket is complete, and whether train/val overlap.

The corresponding implementation is as follows:```python
dataset_checks = [
    {
        "name": "required_files_exist",
        "passed": all(path.exists() for path in REQUIRED_FILES),
    },
    {
        "name": "both_domains_present",
        "passed": {"math", "code"} <= {trace["domain"] for trace in traces},
    },
    {
        "name": "trace_types_present",
        "passed": {"positive", "negative", "repair"} <= {trace["trace_type"] for trace in traces},
    },
    {
        "name": "train_val_no_overlap",
        "passed": not ({record["record_id"] for record in train} & {record["record_id"] for record in val}),
    },
]
```这些检查项把“项目检查”落实为可运行的工程约束。

P06 的流程中，除了 `evaluate_prm.py` 之外，还有 `run_p6_checks.py`。这说明项目不仅关注数据能否生成，还关注代码、产物和报告之间是否一致。

### 15.1 为什么评测不能只看训练后模型表现

项目章节最需要展示的是“工程闭环”，而不是最后模型分数。因为在很多真实团队里，真正最先出问题的不是 PRM 模型本身，而是：

* 某个关键产物漏生成；
* train/val 有重叠；
* 某类 step label 根本没覆盖；
* reward bucket 缺失；
* 代码和报告写的不是同一份数据。

### 15.2 当前项目的检查覆盖说明了什么

现有检查结果显示：

* 总检查项：`10`
* 通过检查项：`10`
* 总体状态：`PASS`
* 命令级检查覆盖：`py_compile, evaluate_prm`
* 数据级检查覆盖：`required_files_exist, both_domains_present, trace_types_present, step_labels_cover_both_classes, reward_buckets_present, train_val_no_overlap ...`

这说明当前项目的检查不是流于形式，而是确实覆盖了代码可运行性、文件存在性、数据分布与训练切分等关键环节。

## 16. 噪声控制：negative 与 repair 轨迹治理

P06 当前最值得深入讨论的，并不是正例轨迹做得多漂亮，而是为什么负例和修复轨迹会成为主要短板。

### 16.1 为什么负例天然更脏

负例轨迹的问题在于，它们往往混合了两种完全不同的成分：

* 有研究价值的“真实错误路径”；
* 没有监督价值的“随机胡写噪声”。

如果项目没有把二者区分开，PRM 很可能学到的是“如何惩罚风格奇怪的文本”，而不是“如何识别真正错误的推理步骤”。

### 16.2 为什么修复轨迹更容易产生歧义

repair 轨迹看起来很理想，因为它模拟了模型犯错后修复的过程。但真正难的是：

* 修复从哪一步开始算；
* 前面错误步骤是否保留；
* 修复后的正确步骤是否要降权；
* 修复轨迹是否会形成风格性偏差。

这些问题如果没有在 schema 和验证层写清楚，repair 数据很快就会成为最不稳定的一类资产。

### 16.3 当前项目给出的明确信号

现有报告已经明确指出，整体通过率的短板主要集中在 negative 和 repair 轨迹，后续优化方向应优先放在 trace 校验与修复轨迹质量控制，而不是盲目扩大规模。

这是一条非常重要的工程结论，因为它把“下一步做什么”从泛泛而谈，收敛成了明确的生产线问题。

## 专题：PRM 数据的标注一致性与 QA 机制

过程监督项目最容易被低估的部分，不是模型调用本身，而是标注一致性。因为结果监督只需要判断“最后对不对”，过程监督却要判断“中间每一步在不在正确轨道上”。这会显著提高标注和 QA 的复杂度。

### 一、为什么 step 级标注更容易出现歧义

对同一道题，两个评审者通常比较容易对最终答案达成一致，却很可能对中间步骤的质量给出不同判断。例如，一步推导可能结果正确，但跳过了关键解释；又或者局部表达不够严谨，但整体方向没有偏离。类似情况在结果监督里影响较小，在 PRM 里却会直接改变 reward 信号。

因此，P06 这类项目的 QA 不能只围绕“有没有错”展开，还必须围绕：

* 这一步是否可被接受为训练信号；
* 这一步的错误属于形式错误、逻辑错误还是可修复错误；
* 这一步是否仍然保留了对后续步骤有价值的信息；
* 这条轨迹是否应该进入 repair 通道，而不是直接丢弃。

把这些问题写清楚，团队才能把 step 级监督从“主观印象”推进到“结构化判断”。

### 二、过程监督更需要分层 QA

相比传统 SFT，PRM 更适合采用分层 QA 机制。比较实用的一种分法是：

* 样本层 QA：检查任务、答案和轨迹三者是否匹配；
* step 层 QA：检查每一步的格式、逻辑连续性和局部正确性；
* 轨迹层 QA：检查 success、negative、repair 三类轨迹是否各自成立；
* 数据集层 QA：检查 reward bucket、任务分布和 train/val 切分是否稳定。

这种分层方式的价值，在于它能帮助团队快速定位噪声来源。否则，一旦发现训练效果不好，所有问题都会被粗暴归因成“PRM 数据不够好”，但没人知道究竟是 step 切分、标签分桶、repair 设计，还是轨迹分布出了问题。

### 三、QA 不只是删错，还要保留“有用的错”

PRM 数据和普通高质量问答数据还有一个重要区别，就是它不应把所有错误过程都视为毫无价值。很多 repair 轨迹恰恰依赖“先犯错，再修正”的结构，才能形成对模型有价值的过程信号。

因此，QA 的目标不应该只是清空错误，而是判断：

* 哪些错误会污染监督，应当剔除；
* 哪些错误虽不正确，但修复路径清晰，适合作为 repair 资产保留；
* 哪些错误可以转化为 failure replay，用于后续规则补强；
* 哪些错误说明 step 切分方式本身需要调整。

一旦团队建立了这种心智，就不会再用“纯净数据集”的思路去误伤 PRM 最有价值的一部分信号。

## 专题：PRM 进入训练系统时的策略选择

P06 已经把过程监督数据做成了训练接口，但真正进入训练时，还会遇到另一个现实问题：这些过程信号究竟应该怎么用。不同团队、不同阶段，对 PRM 数据的使用方式往往差异很大。

### 一、PRM 数据不一定一上来就单独训练

在很多真实项目里，PRM 数据并不会独立承担全部训练任务。更常见的做法，是把它和结果监督数据、SFT 数据或偏好数据一起组成组合式训练策略。原因在于，过程信号擅长塑造推理路径，但未必单独就能覆盖风格、拒答、安全和任务广度。

因此，一个更现实的训练视角通常是：

* 用结果监督保证答案边界；
* 用过程监督增强中间推理质量；
* 用偏好或规则数据约束输出行为；
* 用 smoke 集和 replay 集持续观察回归。

从系统工程角度看，PRM 不是替代一切，而是补上“模型为什么会这样推理”的这一层能力。

### 二、不同阶段适合不同使用方式

在项目早期，PRM 数据更适合作为“结构验证资产”。也就是说，先用它验证 step 切分、reward 逻辑和训练模板是否靠谱，而不是一上来追求大规模收益。随着数据质量提升，再逐步把它放到更重要的位置。

这通常意味着：

* 第一阶段先看数据结构和训练可读性；
* 第二阶段看少量任务上的过程提升是否清晰；
* 第三阶段再考虑扩大任务覆盖和训练占比；
* 第四阶段才讨论是否形成稳定 PRM 训练子系统。

这种渐进方式的价值，在于避免团队在过程信号还不稳定时就做过度投入。

### 三、PRM 项目最终要形成自己的版本语言

当 P06 继续迭代时，团队会越来越需要一套专门描述 PRM 数据版本的语言。例如：

* 本版本扩了哪些任务；
* step 切分规则是否变化；
* reward bucket 是否重定义；
* repair 轨迹占比是否上升；
* 哪些高噪声样本被移出；
* 哪些 replay 问题已被吸收。

只有这些信息被稳定保留，团队才能真正评估“这版 PRM 比上一版好在哪里”。否则，即使训练结果变了，也很难判断变化来自哪一种过程信号调整。对于过程监督这种高度结构化的数据类型来说，版本语言本身就是工程能力的一部分。

---

## 专题：过程监督项目的 replay 集价值

P06 这类项目还有一项很值得长期保留的资产，就是 replay 集。因为过程监督的很多问题，并不会在总体指标里立刻显现，却会在某些典型轨迹上反复出现。只要把这些高频问题沉淀成 replay，团队就能在每轮迭代中更快判断：当前改动到底是在改善真实问题，还是只是让指标看起来更顺。

### 一、replay 集最适合收集哪些问题

对 PRM 来说，最值得放进 replay 集的通常不是随机失败，而是那些会持续破坏过程信号可信度的问题，例如：

* step 切分导致上下文断裂；
* repair 轨迹看似修复，实际只是重写答案；
* 局部步骤合理，但整体方向早已偏离；
* reward bucket 对同类错误给出了不稳定分配。

这些问题一旦被固定下来，就能成为后续规则改动、验证改动和训练模板改动的重要回归样本。

### 二、replay 集能帮助团队建立“问题记忆”

很多过程监督项目最怕的，不是当前有噪声，而是每次都重复踩同样的噪声。replay 集的作用，就是把这些问题从一次性经验，变成可持续复核的项目记忆。只要这份记忆在，团队就更容易回答一个关键问题：这版 P06 真的是更好了，还是只是换了一种方式重复过去的问题。
